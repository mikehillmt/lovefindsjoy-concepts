Love is great. But it isn't enough.

Love can bring two people together. It cannot keep old wounds, protective habits and unspoken needs from running the relationship. That part takes a conscious choice.

For couples who want to grow together, not merely stay together.

You can love each other and still keep hurting each other the same way.

Most couples are not fighting about the dishes, the phone or who said what. They are caught in a pattern that started long before the argument.

You reach for reassurance. Your partner feels controlled.You pull away to protect yourself. Your partner feels abandoned.You try harder to be loved. You lose more of yourself.You keep the peace. Nothing honest gets repaired.

The loop is the relationship you keep having inside the relationship you chose.

Something happens. One of you gets scared. You protect yourself. That protection hits something tender in your partner, so they protect themselves too. Soon you are fighting each other instead of seeing what is happening between you.

01

The trigger

A tone, a silence, a forgotten promise or a need that feels unsafe to say out loud.

02

The protection

You pursue, withdraw, fix, perform, please, control or pretend it does not matter.

03

The collision

Your protection confirms your partner's fear. Their response confirms yours.

04

The choice

You slow the pattern down, tell the truth and decide what you want to create instead.

Mike and Alexis Hill

We met in 2015. The timing was wrong, so nothing happened. Nine years later, one Instagram request turned into a four-and-a-half-hour call. We were married in Montana in 2025.

Before finding each other, Alexis spent twenty-five years inside relationships that kept asking her to carry, perform and become smaller. Mike spent twenty years in a marriage that looked successful while he was starving to feel seen.

We are alcohol-free, neurodivergent and raising a blended family. We bring different histories and different perspectives into the room. We are coaches and guides, not therapists, and we will not pretend that every relationship problem belongs in coaching.

Before we talk

Is this therapy?

No. This is coaching for couples who can participate safely and take responsibility for their choices. We will refer out when therapy or another form of care is more appropriate.

Do we have to be married?

No. You do need a relationship you both want to examine and two willing participants.

Will you tell us whether to stay together?

No. We help you see the pattern and speak more honestly. The authority to choose your relationship stays with you.

Who is this not for?

This is not the right container for active violence, coercive control, unmanaged addiction or an immediate safety crisis. Those situations need specialized support.
