The relationship is not always the problem. Sometimes the pattern is.

You are not fighting about one conversation. You are meeting the same fear through different circumstances.

You pursue.They retreat.
You go quiet.They push harder.
You fix everything.They never learn how to meet you.
You keep performing.Nobody meets the person underneath.

You cannot choose differently until you can see what keeps choosing for you.

We call it the loop. It is the sequence you repeat so often that it starts to feel like the relationship itself.

1Something touches an old fear.
2You protect yourself automatically.
3Your protection touches your partner's fear.
4Both of you defend against each other.
5The pattern wins another argument.

Seeing the loop does not make either person the villain. It gives both of you somewhere honest to begin.

We did not get love right the first time.

Between us are twenty-five years of dating, two long marriages, single parenting, grief, sobriety, blended family life and plenty of patterns we could not see while we were living them.

Mike
"I spent decades pouring into a cup with a hole in it and calling that loyalty."

His loop was performing the good husband, trying harder and using empathy as a reason to stay inside something that required him to disappear.

Alexis
"I chose softer. Not because I'm weak. Because I spent over a decade proving I was strong, and it nearly destroyed me."

Her loop was armor, carrying everything and molding herself into whatever the relationship needed while her own needs went unsaid.

Mike and Alexis Hill

We met in 2015. The timing was wrong. Nine years later, we found each other again.

One Instagram request led to a four-and-a-half-hour call. Twelve days later, Alexis flew to Montana. We were married in 2025 and now raise a blended, neurodivergent family together.

Our histories are not credentials and our marriage is not a promise that we have every answer. They are the reason we refuse to offer perfect-couple advice. We bring two perspectives into the room and help couples tell the truth without turning that truth into a weapon.

We are coaches and guides, not therapists. When a relationship needs clinical care, safety planning or specialized support, we say so.

A few honest answers

Do you decide whether couples should stay together?

No. We help you see what is happening and have the conversations the pattern has been preventing. The decision remains yours.

Is this only for married couples?

No. It is for two people who both want to examine their relationship and are willing to participate.

Is this couples therapy?

No. Coaching can support awareness, communication and conscious choices. It does not replace diagnosis, treatment or crisis care.

When is coaching not appropriate?

Active violence, coercive control, unmanaged addiction and immediate safety concerns require specialized support before couples coaching.
